Best Device to Watch NHL Games

The right device for watching NHL games on IPTV depends on what you already own, but hockey specifically puts more weight on picture quality and motion handling than most sports, since the puck is small, fast, and easy to lose on a blurry stream. Below are the main options, what changes between them across the regular season and playoffs, and how to set each one up.

What actually matters for tracking a fast, small puck

A basketball or a football is a large object moving at a pace a stream can usually keep up with. A hockey puck is a couple of inches wide, moves faster relative to the screen, and changes direction constantly, which means motion blur and dropped frames hurt hockey more than they hurt most other sports. For NHL specifically, the two things worth paying attention to are how consistently a device handles higher-motion video without introducing blur, and how large and sharp the screen itself is, since a small or lower-resolution screen makes a fast puck harder to track regardless of how good the stream is.

Firestick

Amazon's Fire TV Stick is still the cheapest way into IPTV and handles most sports without issue. For hockey, entry-level Firestick models can show more motion blur during fast transitions and odd-man rushes than higher-end hardware, since decoding higher-bitrate, fast-motion video asks more of the chip than a baseball broadcast does. A newer, higher-memory Fire TV Stick handles this noticeably better than the cheapest model.

Android TV boxes

A dedicated Android TV box generally decodes higher-bitrate streams more consistently than a basic Firestick, which shows up most during fast end-to-end rushes and power-play scrambles in front of the net, exactly the moments where losing track of the puck is most costly. If you're a regular hockey viewer, this is one of the clearer upgrades over entry-level streaming sticks.

Smart TV apps

For hockey, the screen itself may matter more than which device is running the app. A larger, sharper smart TV, especially one with a higher refresh rate, makes a fast-moving puck easier to follow than the same stream on a smaller or older display would, regardless of which box or stick is plugged into it. Check our Devices page for which smart TV platforms are currently supported, and consider screen size and refresh rate alongside app support when picking your main hockey-watching setup.

Phones and tablets

A phone or tablet is the toughest place to follow NHL action specifically, since a small screen makes an already small, fast object even harder to track during open play. TruWave TV's app on Android and iOS is genuinely useful for checking a score or catching a replay clip on the go, but it's worth treating as a secondary option rather than your primary way to watch a full hockey game, more so than with sports built around bigger, slower-moving objects.

What to prioritize if you're buying new hardware

If you're deciding what to buy specifically for hockey season, prioritize in this order: a stable connection first, since that affects every device equally, then screen size and sharpness if you're also considering a new TV, then a mid-range or higher streaming device that handles fast motion consistently rather than the cheapest option available. That gap matters most during the playoffs, when a blurry puck during an overtime rush is a worse experience than during a quiet February game.

Setting up for a watch party

If you're hosting rather than watching alone, a single large, sharp screen with a strong connection beats multiple smaller devices spread around a room, more so for hockey than for sports where the action is easier to follow on a smaller display. If the plan is to run the game in more than one room at once, check your plan's simultaneous-connection limit ahead of time. See our multi-device guide for how connection limits work.

Frequently asked questions

What's the best device for watching NHL games on IPTV?

There isn't one universal answer, but for tracking a fast, small puck, a larger and sharper smart TV or a mid-range Android TV box that handles high-motion video consistently tends to work better than an entry-level streaming stick.

Can I watch NHL games on my phone with IPTV?

Yes, TruWave TV's app runs on Android and iOS phones, which is useful for scores and replay clips on the go, though a small screen makes following full games harder for hockey than for most sports.

Do I need a powerful device to stream NHL games smoothly?

Not an extremely powerful one, but for hockey specifically, how consistently a device handles fast motion without blurring matters more than it would for slower-paced sports, since the puck is small and quick to lose track of.

What devices does TruWave TV work on?

Should I use a wired connection for playoff games?

If your device and router support it, yes. A wired connection is more stable than Wi-Fi for high-bitrate sports streams, and during the playoffs, especially a Game 7, dropped frames during a fast rush are a bigger loss than during a regular-season night.

Can I switch devices without losing my subscription?

Yes, your IPTV subscription isn't tied to one device. You can install the app on a new device and log in with the same credentials.
