Best Device to Watch NFL Games

The right device for watching NFL games on IPTV depends on what you already own, but football specifically puts a premium on handling several games at once, since a typical Sunday afternoon has multiple games overlapping in the same two or three hours. Below are the main options, what changes between them on a Sunday with five games running, and how to set each one up.

What actually matters on a Sunday with five games going

Football is concentrated into short, heavy viewing windows, Sunday afternoons especially, where it's common to want a second or third game's score while your main one is still going. That makes two things matter more for football than for a sport with games spread out across the week: whether a device lets you glance at or run another game in a small window without fully leaving your main stream, and how fast it switches channels when you do want to flip over completely, since a slow switch means missing a play during exactly the moment several games are at a key point.

Firestick

Amazon's Fire TV Stick is one of the most common starting points for IPTV, mainly because it's inexpensive and simple to set up, and for a single game on a Thursday or Monday night it's a fine choice. On a Sunday afternoon with several games in progress, its limitation shows: there's no built-in picture-in-picture, so checking another game means backing fully out of your current stream and reloading it when you come back, which costs more time than on some other devices.

Android TV boxes

A dedicated Android TV box varies more by model here, but generally holds recently viewed channels in memory better than a basic Firestick, which makes flipping back and forth between two or three games noticeably faster over the course of a Sunday afternoon. A few boxes also support split-screen apps, though that depends on the specific launcher and isn't universal, so check before assuming it's included.

Smart TV apps

This is where football benefits the most from newer hardware: many current smart TVs support native picture-in-picture across apps, letting you keep your main game full-size while a second game plays in the corner, closer to what a sports bar setup looks like. Support and implementation vary by TV brand and model, so check our Devices page for which smart TV platforms are currently supported before counting on this feature.

Phones and tablets

For football Sundays specifically, a phone works well as a genuine second screen, pulling up a second or third game's score or a key play while the main TV runs your primary game, rather than only as a backup device. TruWave TV's app on Android and iOS also covers you for games you need to catch away from the TV, which comes up more during a condensed holiday-weekend slate than on a typical Sunday.

What to prioritize if you're buying new hardware

If you're deciding what to buy specifically for football season, prioritize in this order: a stable connection first, since that affects every device equally, then picture-in-picture or fast channel-switching if Sunday afternoons with multiple games matter to you, then wired Ethernet if your router and TV's location make that realistic. A device that flips between games quickly will serve a heavy Sunday slate better than one that's only ever tested against a single Thursday night game.

Setting up for a watch party

If you're hosting rather than watching alone, a single large screen with a strong connection, ideally one that can show a second game in picture-in-picture, generally beats scattering several smaller devices around a room each tuned to a different game. If the plan is to run full games in more than one room at once rather than just glancing at a score, check your plan's simultaneous-connection limit ahead of time. Frequently asked questions

What's the best device for watching NFL games on IPTV?

There isn't one universal answer, but if Sunday afternoons with multiple overlapping games matter to you, a smart TV with native picture-in-picture or an Android TV box that switches channels quickly handles that better than a basic streaming stick.

Can I watch NFL games on my phone with IPTV?

Yes, TruWave TV's app runs on Android and iOS phones, which works well both on the go and as a second screen for tracking another game's score while your main TV plays a different one.

Do I need a powerful device to stream NFL games smoothly?

Not an especially powerful one, but for football specifically a device's ability to switch channels quickly or show a second game in picture-in-picture matters more than it would for a sport without multiple simultaneous games to track.

What devices does TruWave TV work on?

TruWave TV's app runs on Firestick, Android TV boxes, Android and iOS phones, and most smart TVs. Check our Devices page for the full compatibility list before you subscribe.

Should I use a wired connection for game day?

If your device and router support it, yes. A wired connection is more stable than Wi-Fi during the busiest Sunday afternoon windows, when your household and your neighbors' are both likely streaming several games at once.

Can I switch devices without losing my subscription?

Yes, your IPTV subscription isn't tied to one device. You can install the app on a new device and log in with the same credentials.

How do I set up TruWave TV on a new device?

What's the best setup for hosting a watch party?

A single large screen with a wired or strong connection, ideally with picture-in-picture for a second game, beats multiple smaller devices for a group watching together. Check your plan's simultaneous-connection limit if you're planning to run games in more than one room at once.
