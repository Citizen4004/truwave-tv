How Much Does It Cost to Watch NBA Without Cable?

"Cut cable, save money" sounds simple until you start adding up what a full NBA season and playoff run actually costs across cable, League Pass, and everything else you watch. Below are real, typical price ranges for each path, framed as approximate since exact pricing shifts year to year, along with where an IPTV subscription like TruWave TV fits into the comparison.

What cable actually costs, beyond the base price

A basic cable or streaming-TV bundle that includes sports channels has generally run somewhere in the $70 to $90 a month range for a lot of providers, and that's often before equipment rental, regional sports network fees, broadcast fees, and taxes get added on top. Promotional pricing is also common, which means the price you signed up for often isn't the price you'll be paying a year in.

What NBA League Pass costs

The league's own streaming package has typically been priced somewhere in the $150 to $200 range for a season of full access to every team, while a single team-specific pass has generally run closer to $120. Local blackout rules still often apply to your own team's home-market games without a separate cable or regional sports subscription, and these figures shift from year to year, so treat them as a typical range rather than this season's exact price.

Hidden costs people forget to count

  • Promotional pricing that expires. A low first-year rate that jumps afterward is common with cable and some streaming bundles alike. Ask what the price looks like after any introductory period.
  • Equipment and rental fees. Cable boxes, DVR rental, and sometimes installation fees add up outside the advertised monthly rate.
  • Regional blackouts on League Pass. Your own team's home-market games may still require a separate subscription even with a full League Pass.
  • Multiple devices or households. If you need more than one screen running at once, check whether that's included or costs extra, regardless of which service you choose.

A realistic example of how the math plays out

Picture a typical setup: a basic streaming-TV bundle at around $80 a month for general channels, plus NBA League Pass averaged out to roughly $15 to $20 a month across the season, plus a separate movie and TV service at $10 to $15 a month. That's somewhere around $105 to $115 a month, before counting any regional blackout gaps or the extended cost of the playoffs. A single TruWave TV subscription at $9.17 a month on the 6-month plan, or $6.67 a month on the 12-month plan, covers live sports channels plus Movies & TV Shows in one subscription, which is the comparison worth making rather than looking at League Pass's price in isolation.

Does switching actually save everyone money?

Not necessarily, and it's worth being honest about that. If you already watch very little TV outside of the NBA season, or you specifically want full League Pass access to every out-of-market team and are happy paying for that directly, the savings from switching may be smaller than the example above. Where the savings show up clearly is for households already paying for a general TV bundle plus League Pass plus a separate movie service, where one subscription replacing that whole stack tends to make the biggest visible difference across an 82-game season and a long playoff run.
