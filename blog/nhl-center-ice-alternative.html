NHL Center Ice Alternative: Is IPTV Worth It?

Center Ice covers out-of-market NHL games across the regular season, and nothing else. For a lot of fans, that's a lot of hockey but still only one piece of what they actually watch all year. Here's a full look at how a broader IPTV subscription compares, where it genuinely wins, and where a dedicated league package still makes sense.

What Center Ice actually covers

Center Ice is a single-purpose add-on built for out-of-market NHL games across the regular season. It doesn't include nationally broadcast games already carried elsewhere, and it doesn't touch anything outside the NHL. That means it's layered on top of whatever you're already using for the rest of your TV.

For a fan who wants exactly that one thing, deep out-of-market NHL coverage, and is otherwise satisfied with their existing TV setup, it does its job well. The friction shows up for people already juggling two, three, or more separate services just to cover everything they watch across a long season, and adding a single-sport package on top only adds another line item rather than simplifying anything.

Why people go looking for an alternative

A few recurring reasons tend to come up when fans start comparing Center Ice against something broader:

  • Subscription stacking. Cable or a base streaming bundle, plus Center Ice, plus whatever else covers movies and other shows, adds up fast and comes from three different bills.
  • Contract and pricing structure. A single-league add-on is priced and billed on its own terms, separate from everything else, which makes the total cost of "watching TV" harder to see at a glance.
  • Limited scope. It only covers the NHL. If you also follow the NFL, NBA, or MLB, that's a separate package or service each time.
  • Device friction. Switching between a league-specific app and whatever else you use for general TV means remembering which app has which content.

Where a broader IPTV subscription differs

NHL coverage sits inside the same Sports category as the rest of your live TV, instead of being billed and managed as a standalone add-on next to a separate cable or streaming bill. For someone trying to simplify down to one app and one monthly cost, that's the core appeal.

A single-purpose sports package covers one league and nothing else. A general Sports category can cover other sports you follow alongside your NHL games, without a separate add-on each time.

Beyond live sports, an IPTV subscription typically includes a Movies & TV Shows library as part of the same plan, so that's one more thing you get without paying for or managing it separately.

What to actually compare before deciding

The honest way to compare the two isn't Center Ice's price against an IPTV plan's price in isolation. It's Center Ice's price plus whatever you're already paying for cable, another streaming bundle, and anything else just to cover the rest of your TV, against one IPTV subscription that covers both the NHL and everything else in one place. For a lot of people, once that full picture is added up, the total looks very different from comparing the two headline prices side by side.

The tradeoff worth knowing

A dedicated league package exists because it's built specifically around out-of-market scheduling for one sport, and that focus is genuinely useful for some fans. If you're already happy with everything else you watch, don't follow other sports closely, and specifically need deep NHL coverage and nothing more, keeping it as a standalone purchase can still be the simpler choice. Where it stops making sense is when you're paying for it and separately paying for several other services just to cover the rest of your TV, which is the exact situation a broader subscription is built to simplify.
