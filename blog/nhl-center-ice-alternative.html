NHL Center Ice Alternative: Is IPTV Worth It?

Subscribing to Center Ice doesn't automatically mean you can watch your own team. The NHL's real coverage problem isn't a blackout rule at all, it's that a lot of teams broadcast home games through their own separate regional sports network app instead.

Why "subscribing" doesn't settle it

Center Ice runs $150 to $180 for the season covering out-of-market games, with pricing shifting slightly year to year, so treat that as approximate. It's layered on top of whatever you already pay for the rest of your TV since it doesn't touch anything outside the NHL. But the games most fans actually care about, their own team's home broadcasts, often don't come from the national out-of-market package at all. They run through a dedicated RSN app specific to that team, and whether you can watch depends entirely on whether you have that particular app, not on whether Center Ice includes you. That's a different kind of problem than a blackout: it's not that the game is withheld, it's that the source carrying it is somewhere else entirely.

What this means in practice

For a fan who mainly wants deep out-of-market coverage and already has their local RSN app sorted separately, Center Ice does exactly what it's built for. The friction shows up for everyone else: paying for Center Ice, then separately tracking down whichever RSN app carries your specific team, then juggling two or three more services just to cover the rest of what you watch. Add in that League scope stops at hockey, so following the NFL, NBA, or MLB means yet another package each time, and the simplification most fans are actually looking for never really arrives.

When Center Ice still covers you fully

If your team's home broadcasts already come through a national package rather than a separate RSN app, or you don't closely track one specific team, Center Ice on its own can be all you need. The calculation changes once you're paying for Center Ice, paying separately for an RSN app, and paying for several more services just to round out the rest of your TV, at which point it stops being one subscription and starts being three or four.

Checking your own team before you commit
