MLB.TV Alternative: Is IPTV Worth It?

A 162-game season is long enough that one bad surprise early on tends to stick with you. For a lot of MLB.TV subscribers, that surprise is finding out their own team's games are blacked out on the service they just paid for.

The blackout rule nobody reads before signing up

MLB.TV covers the full 162-game regular season for about $140 to $150 upfront, or roughly $25 to $30 a month if you join partway through. Pricing moves year to year, so treat those as approximate. On a per-game basis that's under a dollar, which sounds like nothing until you remember it's stacked on top of whatever you're already paying for cable or another streaming bundle for the rest of your TV.

The part that actually trips people up is regional blackouts. If a game involves your home market's team, MLB.TV frequently blacks it out so the local broadcast rights holder keeps that audience. That's not a bug or a limited rollout, it's how the national package is built to work, and it means the exact games most fans signed up to watch are the ones most likely to be missing. If you mostly follow national broadcasts rather than one specific team, you'll likely never notice. If following your own team closely is the point, it's worth finding this out before you pay, not after.

Why a season this long changes the math

A single blackout on a once-a-month playoff game would be an annoyance. Baseball doesn't work that way. Teams play close to every day for six months, so a blackout rule that costs you one game here and there compounds across a season into dozens of missed broadcasts. Add a second or third streaming service running at the same time just to cover the rest of your TV, and the $140 to $150 MLB.TV charge stops being the whole story, it's one line among several, repeating monthly for half a year.

When keeping MLB.TV still makes sense

If you mostly watch national broadcasts, don't follow one team closely enough for local blackouts to bother you, and you're already happy with everything else you watch, there's no real pressure to switch. The blackout rule is a structural feature of that type of package, not a flaw unique to one provider, and it applies the same way regardless of where you stream from. The decision really only matters if your own team's games are the ones you're missing.
