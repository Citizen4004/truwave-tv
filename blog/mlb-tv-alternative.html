MLB.TV Alternative: Is IPTV Worth It?

MLB.TV covers the full 162-game season, but regional blackouts are the catch most people don't find out about until after they've already subscribed and discovered their own team's games aren't included. Here's a full look at how a broader IPTV subscription compares, where it genuinely wins, and where a dedicated league package still makes sense.

What MLB.TV actually covers, and the catch

MLB.TV is a single-purpose add-on covering the 162-game regular season. Its best-known limitation is regional blackouts: if a game involves your home market's team, it's frequently blacked out on the national streaming package in favor of the regional broadcast rights holder, which is the opposite of what most people actually want when they sign up specifically to follow their own team closely. It also doesn't touch anything outside baseball, so it's layered on top of whatever you're already using for the rest of your TV.

For a fan who mostly watches national broadcasts, or whose team's local blackout doesn't affect them, it does its job well across a long season. The friction shows up for people trying to follow one specific team closely while also juggling two or three other services for everything else they watch.

Why people go looking for an alternative

A few recurring reasons tend to come up when fans start comparing MLB.TV against something broader:

  • Blackout frustration. Local blackouts on exactly the games fans want most, their own team, are the single most common complaint about MLB.TV.
  • Subscription stacking. Cable or a base streaming bundle, plus MLB.TV, plus whatever else covers movies and other shows, adds up fast and comes from three different bills.
  • A 162-game season means this matters every week, not just for one or two marquee events a year, so the cumulative friction of blackouts and separate billing adds up over the whole season.
  • Device friction. Switching between a league-specific app and whatever else you use for general TV means remembering which app has which content.

Where a broader IPTV subscription differs

One subscription, not a stack of them. MLB coverage sits inside the same Sports category as the rest of your live TV, instead of being billed and managed as a standalone add-on next to a separate cable or streaming bill. For someone trying to simplify down to one app and one monthly cost, that's the core appeal.

Regional coverage worth confirming upfront, not assuming. Every provider's regional sports network coverage varies by area, so this is the one detail worth checking directly rather than assuming, with any service, including IPTV. Not locked to one league. A single-purpose sports package covers one league and nothing else. A general Sports category isn't limited that way, so the same subscription that covers your MLB games can also cover other sports you follow.

The tradeoff worth knowing

A national streaming package exists because it's built to cover every team broadly, which is exactly why local blackouts happen in the first place, it's a structural tradeoff of that product, not a flaw unique to it. If you mostly watch national broadcasts and don't care about your specific home team's regional games, that tradeoff may not matter to you, and keeping the standalone package can still make sense.
