Best Device to Watch NBA Games

The right device for watching NBA games on IPTV depends on what you already own, but basketball specifically rewards low lag and quick switching more than most sports, since fast breaks and constant stat-checking don't forgive a slow app. Below are the main options, what changes between them for an 82-game season and playoffs, and how to set each one up.

What actually matters for watching basketball

NBA games move in bursts: a fast break and transition dunk happen in seconds, and a lot of viewers also split attention between the TV and a second screen for box scores, other games around the league, or a replay of a call. That combination makes two things matter more for basketball than for a slower-paced sport: how little delay there is between the live action and what shows on your screen, and how quickly a device lets you check something else, a stat, a score, a different game, without losing your place or waiting through a long reload.

Firestick

Amazon's Fire TV Stick is still the simplest and cheapest way into IPTV, and it handles a single NBA game just fine. Where it shows its limits is switching: backing out to check a score or a stat on a basic Firestick often means a full reload of the stream when you come back, which is a bigger interruption during a fast-moving basketball game than it would be during a slower sport. It's still a reasonable starting point if you're mostly watching one game at a time without much second-screen behavior.

Android TV boxes

A dedicated Android TV box generally handles backgrounding and switching between apps more smoothly than a Firestick, since it has more memory to keep the stream app loaded while you check something else. For a sport where fans frequently flip to a stats app or another game mid-quarter, that faster switching adds up over 82 games and a long playoff run.

Smart TV apps

Several newer smart TVs support a native picture-in-picture or multi-view mode that lets you keep the main game in a corner of the screen while checking something else without leaving the app at all, which is close to ideal for basketball's pace. Support varies a lot by brand and model, so check our Devices page for which smart TV platforms are currently supported before assuming yours has this feature.

Phones and tablets

For basketball, a phone is less of a backup option and more of a genuine companion device: pulling up box scores, checking another game's score, or watching a replay of a disputed call while the main TV keeps running is a common pattern during NBA season. TruWave TV's app on Android and iOS also works as your sole screen when you're away from the TV, which matters during a condensed playoff schedule with games most nights of the week.

What to prioritize if you're buying new hardware

If you're deciding what to buy specifically for NBA season, prioritize in this order: a stable connection first, since that affects every device equally, then whichever device switches between apps fastest if you're someone who checks stats or other scores mid-game, then wired Ethernet if your setup allows it. The gap between devices matters most during the playoffs, when a missed fast break while an app reloads is a bigger loss than during a random regular-season Tuesday.

Setting up for a watch party

If you're hosting rather than watching alone, a single large screen with a strong connection beats several smaller devices spread around a room, and it also means only one app needs to handle any second-screen checking rather than every device trying to do it separately. Frequently asked questions

What's the best device for watching NBA games on IPTV?

There isn't one universal answer, but if you regularly switch to check stats or another score mid-game, a device with native picture-in-picture or faster app-switching, like a newer smart TV or an Android TV box, handles basketball's pace better than a basic streaming stick.

Can I watch NBA games on my phone with IPTV?

Yes, TruWave TV's app runs on Android and iOS phones, which works well both as your main screen on the go and as a second screen for stats and scores while the TV plays the game.

Do I need a powerful device to stream NBA games smoothly?

Not an especially powerful one, but for basketball specifically a device that switches quickly between the stream and other apps matters more than it would for a slower-paced sport, especially during fast breaks you don't want to miss.

Should I use a wired connection for playoff games?

If your device and router support it, yes. A wired connection is more stable than Wi-Fi during the playoffs and Finals, when a lot of households are streaming at once and a dropped frame during a fast break is a bigger loss than during a regular-season game.

Can I switch devices without losing my subscription?

Yes, your IPTV subscription isn't tied to one device. You can install the app on a new device and log in with the same credentials.
