Best Device to Watch Live Sports on IPTV

The right device for watching sports on IPTV depends on what you already own, but which features actually matter changes depending on which sport you follow. Football rewards handling several overlapping games at once. Basketball rewards fast app-switching. Baseball rewards a device that holds up night after night for six months. Hockey rewards clean motion handling and screen quality. Below is what to prioritize for each, a combined comparison, and how to set up whichever device you pick.

Why "best device" depends on the sport

A device that feels perfectly fine for one sport can show real limits with another. The differences come from how each sport is actually watched, not from raw processing power:

  • NFL: a Sunday afternoon packs several overlapping games into two or three hours, so picture-in-picture and fast channel-switching matter more than for a sport spread evenly across the week.
  • NBA: fast breaks happen in seconds, and a lot of fans split attention between the game and a second screen for stats or other scores, so low lag and quick app-switching matter most.
  • MLB: 162 games played close to every day for six months means the real test is whether a device holds a stream open through a long game and wakes back up cleanly after sitting idle, not how it handles any single night.
  • NHL: the puck is small, fast, and easy to lose on a blurry stream, so motion handling and screen size and sharpness matter more here than for any other major sport.

Firestick

Amazon's Fire TV Stick is the cheapest and simplest way into IPTV, and it's a fine choice across all four sports for a single game watched start to finish. Its limits show up differently by sport: no built-in picture-in-picture means backing fully out to check another NFL game, a slower reload when flipping to check an NBA score mid-quarter, and entry-level models with less memory can get a streaming app closed in the background during a long MLB game or a hockey intermission. A newer, higher-memory Fire TV Stick handles all of this noticeably better than the cheapest model on the shelf.

Android TV boxes

A dedicated Android TV box generally has more memory and handles backgrounding and app-switching more smoothly than a Firestick. That pays off differently by sport: faster flipping between overlapping NFL games, quicker backgrounding to check NBA stats mid-quarter, more consistent decoding of high-bitrate NHL streams during fast rushes, and fewer force-closes across a long MLB season. If you follow more than one sport seriously, this is usually the first upgrade worth making over a basic streaming stick.

Smart TV apps

Many current smart TVs support native picture-in-picture or multi-view, which is close to ideal for football and basketball specifically, keeping a second game or a stats view visible without leaving your main stream. For hockey, the screen itself often matters more than the device: a larger, sharper display with a higher refresh rate makes a fast-moving puck easier to track than the same stream on an older or smaller TV. For baseball, a smart TV's app sleep and wake behavior is tied to the TV's own power management, which tends to hold up reliably across a long season. Support and feature sets vary by brand and model, so check our Devices page for which smart TV platforms are currently supported.

Phones and tablets

A phone is a genuine second screen for football and basketball, tracking another game's score or a stat while your main TV runs a different one, and a useful way to catch a day game while you're out during baseball season. For hockey, a small screen is the toughest place to follow fast open play, so treat a phone as a way to check scores and replay clips rather than your primary hockey setup. What to prioritize if you're buying new hardware

Across every sport, a stable internet connection matters more than the device itself, so start there regardless of what you watch. After that, prioritize by what you follow: picture-in-picture or fast switching if you're an NFL or NBA fan who flips between games or checks stats mid-action, a device with enough memory to stay open through long sessions if you follow baseball closely, and screen size and motion handling if hockey is your main sport. Wired Ethernet helps every sport equally if your setup makes it realistic.

Setting up for a watch party

Whichever sport you're hosting for, a single large screen with a strong connection, ideally with picture-in-picture if you're tracking a second game, generally beats scattering several smaller devices around a room. Frequently asked questions

Is there one best device for every sport?

No. A basic Firestick is fine for a single Thursday night NFL game but shows its limits during an NBA back-to-back or a 162-game MLB season. What a device needs to handle well changes by sport, not just by budget.

What devices does TruWave TV work on?

TruWave TV's app runs on Firestick, Android TV boxes, Android and iOS phones, and most smart TVs. Check our Devices page for the full compatibility list before you subscribe.

Do I need a powerful device to stream sports smoothly?

Not an especially powerful one for most sports. What matters more is sport-specific: fast app-switching for basketball, memory that holds a stream open for a long baseball season, and strong motion handling for hockey.

Can I switch devices without losing my subscription?

Yes, your IPTV subscription isn't tied to one device. You can install the app on a new device and log in with the same credentials.
