NBA League Pass Alternative: Is IPTV Worth It?

League Pass actually comes in two different versions, and the cheaper one has a catch that only shows up once your team lands a big national broadcast you can't watch.

Two passes, two different sets of blackouts

Full-league League Pass runs $150 to $200 a season and skips every game that's nationally televised, no matter which teams are playing. A team-specific pass costs less, closer to $120, but goes a step further: it also blacks out your own team's national broadcasts. Both figures move around year to year, so treat them as approximate. The practical effect is that the cheaper option can leave you without the exact marquee matchups you bought the pass to see, which is the opposite of how most people assume a cheaper, team-focused plan should work.

Either version is layered on top of whatever you already pay for the rest of your TV, since League Pass doesn't touch anything outside the NBA. For a fan who only cares about deep out-of-market coverage and is fine with occasional national-game gaps, it does what it's built to do. The friction shows up once you're also juggling two or three other services just to cover everything else you watch, and a single-sport add-on becomes one more bill rather than a simpler setup.

Why basketball specifically pushes people to look elsewhere

Part of it is the math: $150 to $200 a season for full access, or around $120 for a pass that still excludes your team's biggest games, stacked on top of cable or a streaming bundle covering movies and everything else. Part of it is scope, League Pass only covers the NBA, so following the NFL, MLB, or the NHL means a separate package each time. And part of it is just the back-and-forth of switching apps mid-evening, since basketball's stop-start rhythm, timeouts, free throws, replay reviews, creates constant moments where someone checks a score elsewhere and expects to land right back in the game.

The real comparison is that price, plus whatever you already pay for cable or another bundle just to cover the rest of your TV, against one subscription that covers both. When a team pass still makes sense

If you're already satisfied with everything else you watch, only care about the NBA, and don't mind the occasional national-broadcast gap, keeping League Pass as a standalone purchase is a reasonable choice on its own. Where it stops making sense is paying for a single-sport pass, then separately paying for two or three other services just to round out the rest of your TV, which is usually what pushes people toward a broader subscription instead. Worth checking before you commit either way: look up how many of your team's games landed on national broadcasts last season, since that number tells you exactly how much the cheaper team pass would have actually cost you in missed games.
